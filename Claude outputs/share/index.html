Design presentation

Homepage design routes for the Genedrift website revamp.

Two complete routes plus one concept study. Every screen below is a live page — open it in a browser and the animations, maps and interactive states run exactly as they would on the finished site.

Approach

How these three routes came about

Three documents set the constraints: the LF20 Website Design Philosophy, the LF20 presentation itself, and the Final Information Architecture, Sitemap & Vendor Development Brief. Everything here is built from those.

All copy, figures and market data come from Genedrift's own material — the 1,000+ submissions, 500+ PV deliverables and the capability matrix on page 12 of the LF20 deck. Nothing is invented, and anything still missing is marked in brackets rather than filled with placeholder text.

Rather than present one interpretation, I built three. Each answers a different question, and they are deliberately not equivalent — one is the recommendation, one is a comparison, one is a demonstration.

Route A

Does the brief work?

Built strictly to the LF20 philosophy — approved palette, neo-grotesque typography, rounded card system, circular line icons, purple and grey map coding, large purple numerals on lavender. All twenty non-negotiable vendor rules respected: no new colours, no gradients, no heavy shadows, no mixed icon styles. Structure follows the thirteen homepage blocks the sitemap brief specifies.

This is the recommended route.

Route B

What if the visual system were open?

A different register — neutral off-white, near-black and the Genedrift purple accent, photography-led, hairline grids instead of cards. It positions Genedrift closer to a global consulting or legal practice than to a pharma services vendor.

It does not follow the LF20 palette and would need approval as a design-system deviation under section 19. Content, sequence and information architecture are identical to Route A, so the two can be judged on visual direction alone.

Concept study

Where is the ceiling?

Not a proposal — a demonstration of what the brand could support if Genedrift ever wanted a site that competes on craft. The idea comes from the name: genetic drift is what happens to a population as it spreads into new territory, which is precisely the business.

Included so the range of the identity is visible, and so the safer routes are chosen deliberately rather than by default.

A note on the three heroes in Route A

The hero is the one place the LF20 philosophy allows real expression — section 6 permits abstract, geometric and geographic graphics and the circular line motif as a signature device. Three treatments are shown because each makes a different argument: Orbit turns the deck's own concentric line motif into a diagram of the six regions and forty-six markets; Pathway visualises the twelve-stage integrated service architecture as one continuous line; Regulatory Pulse leads with geography, the strongest single proof point. Any of the three can be swapped into the homepage without affecting another section.

How to view these

  1. Open any card above — each is a real web page, not an image. Animations run on load.
  2. View at full width. The desktop pages are designed at 1440px; maximise the browser window.
  3. Give the animated pages a moment. The map on Hero A3 cycles through six regions over about fifteen seconds; the concept page runs a thirty-second cycle.
  4. Bracketed text such as [DATE] marks content still to be supplied.

Decisions and outstanding items

Three decisions unblock the build; the rest is content.

Decisions needed from this review

  1. Route. A or B. If B, it needs written approval as a design-system deviation.
  2. Coverage tool. Whether to publish market status openly, including markets where coverage is not yet available. The tool depends on it.
  3. Hero treatment. Orbit, Pathway or Regulatory Pulse. Orbit is currently built into the Route A homepage.
  4. Photography. Whether to commission a shoot. Route B depends on it; Route A does not.

Content still required

  1. Registered address and telephone number for the footer and contact page.
  2. Knowledge Hub records — featured article, latest regulatory updates, country intelligence, whitepaper and market entry guide.
  3. Market count confirmation. The LF20 deck states 30+ markets supported; the page 12 capability matrix lists 46 across APAC (15), Africa (17), CIS (4), Eastern Europe (2), Middle East (2) and Latin America (6). These designs use “46 mapped, 30+ live”.
  4. Regional positioning. Latin America and Eastern Europe appear in the capability matrix but not in the positioning line, which reads APAC, Africa, Middle East and CIS. Please confirm which is correct.
  5. Photography direction, if Route B is selected.
